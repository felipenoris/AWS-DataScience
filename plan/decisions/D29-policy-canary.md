# D29 — Where a Service Control Policy is tested before it reaches anything real

**Status:** Decided (2026-08-08): **a tenth account, `Policy Canary`, alone in a fifth OU, `Policy Test`**

**In one line:** A tenth account, `Policy Canary`, alone in a fifth OU, `Policy Test` — because an empty OU tests nothing.

**Related decisions:** [D20](D20-staging-account.md), [D23](D23-ou-structure.md)

**Referenced by stages:** [Stage 1a](../stages/stage-01a-landing-zone.md), [Stage 1b](../stages/stage-01b-identity-and-controls.md)

## Rationale and consequences

Stage 1b step 7 used to end with "apply these to a test OU first", and no such OU existed — D23 chose four and `plan/institutional-delta.md` recorded the Policy Staging OU as the notable absence. So the only stated safety procedure for the most dangerous step in the plan was unexecutable. **The obvious fix does not work, and understanding why is what shapes this decision: an *empty* OU tests nothing.** An SCP is a permission ceiling evaluated when a principal makes a call; with no account under the OU there is no principal, so attaching a candidate policy there proves only that the JSON parsed and that Organizations accepted the attachment — both of which `create-policy` already told you. A policy staging OU is a test **only if it contains a throwaway account**. Hence the account, not just the OU. **The two failure classes it has to catch, because intuition only covers the first:** *too tight* — the policy denies something legitimate (the archetype is a region SCP that forgets the global services answering in `us-east-1`; in this plan's own set, the `aws:ResourceOrgID` deny that forgets the AWS-owned buckets of `plan/architecture.md` §4.2), which fails loudly and immediately; and *too loose* — the condition never matches and the policy denies nothing, which fails **silently** and leaves a control that exists only on paper. The second is Lessons 5 and 13 in `CLAUDE.md`, and it is the one an empty OU can never catch. The test battery therefore runs from **both** directions: the calls that must fail, and the calls that must still succeed. **What the account holds: nothing.** No Terraform slice, no `bootstrap/`, no state bucket, no data, no VPC. It is not one of the Terraform-managed accounts and gains no `awsds-infra-*` profile; SCP attachment is an Organizations call made from Management (principle 1). **What it does need, and it is the part that is easy to get wrong: the test principal must be an administrator.** An SCP sets the ceiling; testing it with a restricted principal measures the identity policy instead, and a deny that a non-administrator could not have exercised anyway proves nothing. So `AdministratorAccess` is assigned to the `infrastructure` user on this account and on no other basis. **Which axis it sits on:** neither. Like Management, Log Archive, Audit and Identity, it is a **platform** account — the organization's own machinery, serving every account and belonging to no environment (`Environment=shared`). **Naming, deliberately not the industry term.** The literature calls this a *Policy Staging OU*; this plan warns three separate times (D20, D23, Stage 1a step 4) that the name collides with the `Staging` **account** while the concepts do not. Creating a console object called `Policy Staging` next to an account called `Staging` would resurrect exactly that ambiguity, so the OU is `Policy Test` and the account is `Policy Canary` — the thing that breaks first so that nothing else does. **Cost:** one more AWS Config recorder, ~USD 0.50-1/month; no KMS key, since it encrypts nothing — it adds one governed account to the Config row in `plan/cost-model.md`. **It also consumes an Organizations account slot**, which matters because the organization's account quota starts low (Stage 1a pre-flight). **What was considered and rejected:** (i) an empty OU — free and worthless, for the reason above; (ii) using the `Interactive` OU as the staging OU while Sandbox and Development are still empty — genuinely free and a real test, but valid only until Stage 3 puts something in those accounts, i.e. a solution with an expiry date that nobody would remember to honour. **Revision trigger:** if a second policy-testing need appears (a `Suspended` OU, a second throwaway account for RCP testing), `Policy Test` stops being a single-account OU by itself; until then it is the one OU in this plan whose *purpose* is to contain a disposable account, which is why it passes the D23 test that a single-account OU normally fails.
